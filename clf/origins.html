Our Origins

Before there was an organization, there was an example.

Community Life Forward grew out of a way of living I learned at the side of Imam W. Deen Mohammed, whom I served as assistant for the last ten years of his life.

Saafir Rabb II, Founder & Chair

The Arc

Every institution inherits a temperament from the people who formed its founders. Ours was formed by moral courage, by service, and by the conviction that faith is proven in public life. This is where that understanding began.

The Teacher

Imam W. Deen Mohammed

In 1975, upon the passing of his father, the Honorable Elijah Muhammad, Imam W. Deen Mohammed assumed leadership of the Nation of Islam. He then led its members into Islam as it is practiced by Muslims around the world. It was one of the most consequential religious transformations in American life, and he carried it out by teaching, not by force.

He had been a confidant of Malcolm X. He became the spiritual leader of Muhammad Ali and of the countless men and women who made the transition of 1975 alongside him. His importance to the arc of Muslim American community life cannot be overstated.

What he modeled was a method: Muslims standing as morally courageous, service-oriented, civically engaged participants in the life of this country. That method is the foundation Community Life Forward is built on.

  1. 1933Born in Michigan, the son of Elijah Muhammad.
  2. 1975Succeeds his father and leads the community to universal Islam.
  3. 1998Saafir Rabb II begins ten years of service as his assistant.
  4. 2008Imam Mohammed transitions. His method lives on in the work.

Ten Years at His Side

What I learned by watching him work.

For the last ten years of his life, I served as Imam Mohammed's assistant. I traveled with him, sat in rooms with him, and watched how he carried the weight of a community. Three lessons from those years became the ethos of everything we do.

  1. I.

    Moral courage

    Imam Mohammed said what was true when it was costly to say it. He changed the course of our faith trajectory when the truth required it. He also unified our identity with the Muslim world by prioritizing our human identity over all other parts of our identity.

  2. II.

    Service

    Leadership, as he practiced it, was measured by what reached people, not by who stood at the podium.

  3. III.

    Civic engagement

    He taught that faith is proven in public: in neighborhoods, schools, workplaces and the life of the nation.

The Anchor

Louis Omar

Louis Omar served as an assistant to Malcolm X, alongside Louis Farrakhan. The two were known as “the two Louis.” He carried the living memory of the movement's most demanding years.

To me, he was a grandfather; he was present from my earliest years. Along with my own father, who served as his captain in the 1960s, he was a constant anchor for my guidance coming up. When I needed direction, he gave it plainly. When I drifted, he steadied me.

If Imam Mohammed showed me what a community could become, Louis Omar showed me what it takes to keep showing up for one.

I did not inherit a program. I inherited a posture: stand up straight, tell the truth, and go to work for your neighbors.

Saafir Rabb II

From Inheritance to Institution

How the example became Community Life Forward.

Community Life Forward is a Baltimore-based 501(c)(3) devoted to civic engagement, freedom, justice and community health. Each part of our work puts the lessons above into practice.

    Service

    Showing up where the need is: community health, justice and the everyday work of neighbors caring for neighbors.
